# TISH inspection in Minneapolis and Saint Paul

> Last updated 2026-11-01. Checked against the official City of Minneapolis and City of Saint Paul pages listed at the end of this page.

Reviewed by MN Home Sale Pros staff

A TISH (Truth-in-Sale of Housing) inspection is a city-required evaluation of a home before it is marketed for sale. In Minneapolis and Saint Paul, it is done by a private evaluator licensed by the city, who sets their own fee. Minneapolis can require repairs. Saint Paul's report is mostly disclosure.

## How do Minneapolis and Saint Paul compare?

|  | Minneapolis | Saint Paul |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who needs one | Single-family houses, duplexes, townhouses, first-time condo conversions | Single-family, duplex, condominium and townhome |
| When | Full report before the property can be shown | Report available to buyers within 3 calendar days of listing |
| How long it is valid | Two years or one sale | One year (365 days), one sale |
| Required repairs | Yes. Listed repairs must be completed (by the seller, or by the buyer within 90 days of closing) | Disclosure only. The only required item is an operational hard-wired smoke detector in single-family homes |
| Who sets the fee | Each licensed evaluator | Each licensed evaluator or their employer |

Sources: [Minneapolis TISH](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/property-housing/buying-selling/tish/), [Minneapolis evaluations](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/property-housing/buying-selling/tish/evaluations/), [Saint Paul TISH](https://www.stpaul.gov/departments/safety-inspections/rent-buy-sell-property/selling-or-buying-properties/truth-sale-housing).

## What does a TISH evaluator check?

The evaluator looks at the condition of the building inside and out, including structure, roof, windows and doors, heating, plumbing, electrical, gas piping, water heater, smoke and CO detectors, stairs, egress and sleeping rooms.

Minneapolis's checklist also covers attic insulation, chimneys, drainage, foundation, garage, floor drains, sanitation and evidence of dampness or vermin. Evaluators look at some items that do not need repair, such as chipped or peeling paint, holes in walls, roofs, and 30 or 60-amp electrical service that has not been tampered with. They do not evaluate non-essential systems (wood stoves, fireplaces, air conditioners) or toxic substances such as asbestos, lead-based paint, formaldehyde or radon ([Minneapolis checklist](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/property-housing/buying-selling/tish/evaluation-checklist/)).

Because radon and lead paint are not part of TISH, buyers who want them checked need separate tests. See [radon testing in Minnesota](https://mnhomesalepros.com/radon-testing-minnesota/).

## How can a seller prepare?

Minneapolis suggests checking its list of commonly required repairs and fixing what you can before the evaluation, so those items do not appear on the report.

Common no-permit fixes on the Minneapolis list include GFCI outlets that will not trip, a missing cleanout plug, a toilet ballcock that is not anti-siphon, and missing backflow valves on threaded laundry and outdoor faucets. Smoke detectors are required outside each separate sleeping area, and CO detectors within 10 feet of a sleeping room. Some repairs, such as boiler or water heater relief valves, flues, or new gas appliances, need a permit ([Minneapolis evaluations](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/property-housing/buying-selling/tish/evaluations/); [Minneapolis checklist](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/property-housing/buying-selling/tish/evaluation-checklist/)).

## What happens after the report in Minneapolis?

The evaluator files the report online. The seller must display it at showings. If repairs are listed, they must be completed before the City issues a Certificate of Approval.

1. No required repairs, or repairs completed by the seller: the City issues a Certificate of Approval online.
1. Repairs not done by closing: the buyer signs an acknowledgment of responsibility, files it within one day of closing and completes the repairs within 90 days.
1. Reinspection: work done under a permit is reinspected by the City inspector named on the permit, at no extra city charge. Work without a permit is reinspected by a licensed evaluator, who sets their own fee. Safety checks are done by licensed contractors.

Sources: [Minneapolis evaluations](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/property-housing/buying-selling/tish/evaluations/), [Minneapolis reinspections](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/property-housing/buying-selling/tish/re-inspections/). A pre-inspection report does not replace the disclosure report.

## What else should Saint Paul sellers know?

If a single-family home has no working hard-wired smoke detector, the report marks it as a hazard and a licensed electrical contractor must install one under a permit.

A sale to immediate family does not need a TISH. A valid Certificate of Code Compliance or current Certificate of Occupancy can be accepted instead. Failing to comply with Chapter 189 is a misdemeanor. The City also says buyers are best served by hiring their own independent home inspector ([Saint Paul TISH](https://www.stpaul.gov/departments/safety-inspections/rent-buy-sell-property/selling-or-buying-properties/truth-sale-housing)).

## How do I find a TISH evaluator?

Each city publishes its own list of licensed evaluators. Neither city recommends a particular evaluator.

Minneapolis: use the city's [evaluator finder](http://apps.ci.minneapolis.mn.us/evaluatorfinderapp/evaluatorfinderrpt.aspx) (TISH office 612-673-5840). Saint Paul: we list the city's **37** approved evaluators on our [Saint Paul TISH page](https://mnhomesalepros.com/truth-in-sale-of-housing/saint-paul/). For other cities with point-of-sale rules, see [TISH in Minnesota](https://mnhomesalepros.com/truth-in-sale-of-housing/).

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does a TISH inspection cost?**

Neither city sets the price. In both Minneapolis and Saint Paul, evaluators are private and set their own fees, so compare quotes.

**Does TISH check for radon or lead paint?**

No. Minneapolis evaluators do not evaluate toxic substances such as asbestos, lead-based paint, formaldehyde or radon.

**How long is a TISH report valid?**

In Minneapolis, two years or one sale. In Saint Paul, one year (365 days) and one sale.

**Who makes TISH repairs in Minneapolis if the house is sold first?**

The buyer signs an acknowledgment of responsibility, files it within one day of closing and must finish the required repairs within 90 days of closing.
