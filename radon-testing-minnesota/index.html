Radon testing in Minnesota

Last updated 2026-11-01. Checked against the official MDH, state statute and city sources listed at the end of this page. Business counts come from our listing records, built from the MDH Radon Service Providers list retrieved 2026-10-12.

Reviewed by MN Home Sale Pros staff

The Minnesota Department of Health (MDH) recommends testing every Minnesota home for radon. Start with a short-term test, in the heating season if you can, on the lowest level of the home you use regularly. Follow MDH's decision steps before deciding whether to fix it.

Why test for radon in Minnesota?

MDH says the average radon level in Minnesota is more than three times the U.S. level, and more than two in five Minnesota homes have levels that pose a significant health risk.

MDH also estimates that 21,000 people die each year in the United States from lung cancer caused by radon, and says a test is the only way to know a home's level (MDH).

Short-term or long-term test?

Start with a short-term test, which usually runs 2 to 7 days. A long-term test runs at least 90 days and gives the best estimate of the yearly average.

Long-term tests should cover both heating and non-heating seasons. MDH recommends retesting every 2 to 5 years, and also after mitigation and after major changes to the home (MDH testing).

How do I set up a test correctly?

Put the kit on the lowest regularly used level, at least 20 inches above the floor, and keep the house closed for any test shorter than 3 months.

  • Where: the lowest level you use regularly. MDH says to test the basement if you spend more than 10 hours a week there. In real estate, test the lowest level, such as the basement.
  • Placement: at least 20 inches above the floor, 1 foot from exterior walls, 3 feet from exterior windows, and away from drafts.
  • Closed-house conditions: keep windows and exterior doors closed except for normal entry and exit, and run heating or cooling as usual.

Source: MDH radon testing.

What do the results mean?

MDH recommends fixing the home if your follow-up result or average is 4 pCi/L or higher, and considering it at 2 to 3.9 pCi/L.

First short-term resultMDH next step
0 to 1.9 pCi/LRetest every 2 to 5 years
2 to 7.9 pCi/LDo a long-term follow-up test
8 pCi/L or higherDo a short-term follow-up test

After the follow-up test, MDH recommends mitigation at 4 pCi/L or higher and says to consider it at 2 to 3.9 pCi/L. Complete two tests before deciding on mitigation. The exception is a professional using a continuous radon monitor in a real estate transaction (MDH testing).

Can I test it myself?

Yes, for your own home. MDH recommends a licensed professional when you need an unbiased third party, for example in a home sale.

Under the Radon Licensing Act, anyone who tests or mitigates for radon in a building they do not own or lease must be licensed by MDH (MDH; Minn. Stat. § 144.4961). Consumer digital radon monitors usually cost about $100 to $200. MDH notes they have not been comprehensively evaluated or approved, and does not recommend them for real estate or mitigation decisions (MDH). MDH lists Air Chek short-term kits, which come with a discount for Minnesota residents (MDH).

How does radon testing work in a home sale?

Minnesota does not require a radon test to sell a home, but the seller must disclose what they know about radon, and MDH highly recommends testing.

Under the Minnesota Radon Awareness Act, before the purchase agreement is signed, the seller must disclose any known radon test results and mitigation details and give the buyer MDH's radon publication (Minn. Stat. § 144.496). For tests during a sale, MDH says a licensed professional should test for at least 48 hours. One option is a calibrated continuous radon monitor; the other is two short-term kits placed 4 inches apart and averaged. Each foundation type in the home should be tested (MDH real estate guidance).

How do I find a licensed tester?

MDH publishes the list of licensed radon service providers. Ask any tester for a copy of their MDH licence.

Our records list 234 MDH-licensed businesses offering radon measurement and 14 licensed radon labs, grouped from the MDH Radon Service Providers list. Browse them on our radon page or by county, or request quotes. MDH suggests asking whether a continuous monitor has been calibrated within the past year (MDH).

Frequently asked questions

How long does a radon test take?

A short-term test usually takes 2 to 7 days. A long-term test takes at least 90 days. In a home sale, MDH says a professional test runs at least 48 hours.

What radon level is too high in Minnesota?

MDH recommends mitigation at 4 pCi/L or higher and says to consider it at 2 to 3.9 pCi/L.

Is a radon test required to sell a house in Minnesota?

No. The Radon Awareness Act requires the seller to disclose known radon test results and mitigation details and give the buyer MDH's radon publication, but testing is not required.

Can I use a digital radon monitor?

For your own information, yes. MDH does not recommend consumer digital monitors for real estate transactions or mitigation decisions.
