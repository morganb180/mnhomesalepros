# Radon mitigation in Minnesota

> Last updated 2026-11-01. Checked against the official MDH, state statute and city sources listed at the end of this page. Business counts come from our listing records, built from the MDH Radon Service Providers list retrieved 2026-10-12.

Reviewed by MN Home Sale Pros staff

If testing shows radon at 4 pCi/L or higher, MDH recommends a mitigation system. MDH says a system generally costs $1,500 to $3,000. It must be installed by an MDH-licensed mitigation professional and carry an MDH system tag.

## When should I install a radon mitigation system?

MDH recommends mitigation when the follow-up test or average is 4 pCi/L or higher, and says to consider it at 2 to 3.9 pCi/L.

MDH's goal for a system is to get radon below 4 pCi/L, and it notes that a quality system may reduce year-round levels below 2 pCi/L ([MDH testing](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/radontestresults.html); [MDH systems](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/mitigationsystem.html)). See [radon testing in Minnesota](https://mnhomesalepros.com/radon-testing-minnesota/) for the testing steps.

## How much does radon mitigation cost in Minnesota?

MDH says costs generally range from $1,500 to $3,000, depending on the type of system and how the home was built.

MDH warns against unlicensed installers and very cheap quotes (under $1,000), which it says often lead to poor workmanship and materials. MDH also advises against paying in full until a valid radon test shows reduced levels ([MDH systems](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/mitigationsystem.html); [MDH hiring guidance](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/mitigation.html)). An active system costs slightly more than $14 a year to run, on average, according to MDH ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/radonresistant.html)).

## What types of systems are there?

MDH names three common types: sub-slab suction, drain tile suction and sub-membrane (for crawl spaces).

- **Sub-slab suction** pulls radon from beneath the foundation and vents it outside.
- **Drain tile suction** taps into the drain tile, with covers placed on sump baskets.
- **Sub-membrane** seals a plastic sheet over a dirt crawl-space floor and pulls soil gas from beneath it.

Source: [MDH radon mitigation systems](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/mitigationsystem.html).

## What should a finished system include?

A fan in an unconditioned space, a U-tube manometer, an MDH system tag and a PVC vent pipe that meets MDH's height and distance rules.

- The fan goes in an unconditioned space such as an attic, garage or outside, never in or below living space.
- A U-tube manometer shows whether the fan is working. MDH also lists an active notification monitor that alarms if the fan fails.
- The licensed professional attaches an MDH system tag with the installer's name, phone, install date and licence number.
- The vent pipe must be at least 10 feet above ground, 10 feet from windows, doors and other openings (unless it vents at least 2 feet above them), and above the roof edge.
- A post-mitigation test should be done at least 24 hours after the system starts running.
- Most installs need electrical work for the fan, which requires an electrical permit. Questions go to the Department of Labor and Industry (651-284-5026).

Source: [MDH radon mitigation systems](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/mitigationsystem.html).

## Does the installer need a licence?

Yes. State law has required an annual MDH licence for radon mitigation for pay since January 1, 2019. Systems installed on or after that date must carry an MDH tag.

See the Radon Licensing Act ([Minn. Stat. § 144.4961](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/144.4961)). MDH says homeowners can request a free MDH inspection of professionally installed systems in existing buildings installed since June 1, 2020 ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/radonre.html)).

## How do I hire a mitigation contractor?

Hire only an MDH-licensed mitigation professional. Make sure they visit the house before bidding, and do not pay in full until a post-mitigation test shows lower levels.

MDH says bids should not be made over the phone or by email ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/mitigation.html)). ## Is there financial help?

MDH lists programs that may help, depending on household income, location and funding.

They include Minnesota Housing home improvement loan programs, USDA Rural Development repair loans and grants, and the Minnesota Homeownership Center's directory of local programs ([MDH financial assistance](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/financial.html)).

## Do new Minnesota homes already have a system?

Since 2009, new Minnesota homes must be built radon-resistant. That means sealed entry points and a vent pipe, but no fan (a passive system). MDH recommends testing every Minnesota home.

Adding a fan turns a passive system into an active one, which MDH says removes radon better ([MDH radon-resistant construction](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/radonresistant.html); [MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/air/radon/index.html)).

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does radon mitigation cost in Minnesota?**

MDH says costs generally range from $1,500 to $3,000, depending on the type of system and how the home was built.

**Who can install a radon system in Minnesota?**

Only an MDH-licensed radon mitigation professional can install one in a building they do not own or lease. Industry certification alone is not enough.

**How do I know the system works?**

Check that the manometer shows the fan is running and that an MDH tag is attached. Do a post-mitigation test at least 24 hours after the system starts, and retest every 2 to 5 years.

**Is help available to pay for radon mitigation?**

MDH lists Minnesota Housing loan programs, USDA Rural Development repair loans and grants, and the Minnesota Homeownership Center directory. Eligibility depends on income, location and funding.
